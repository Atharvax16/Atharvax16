## 🔬 About my research
 
I'm an MSc Computing (AI) student at **Dublin City University**, supervised by **Prof. Alessandra Mileo**, working on generative AI-based retinal image restoration for diabetic retinopathy (DR) screening.
 
My central finding — a **"diagnostic paradox"** — is that generative restoration models can improve pixel-level image quality (PSNR/SSIM) while *failing to reliably recover, or even degrading,* diagnostic accuracy. This work is currently **under review at OMIA/MICCAI 2026**.
 
Broadly, I care about a question that spans imaging, generative models, and detection:
 
> **Does a model actually work — or does it just look like it does?**
 
That question shows up across my current projects: diagnostic reliability in restored medical images, distinguishing AI-generated from real images, and using model trustworthiness/calibration signals rather than surface-level metrics as ground truth.

## 🧪 Current research threads
 
- **Generative restoration for DR screening** — benchmarking Cold Diffusion, SwinIR+GAN, Conditional DDPM, CLAHE, Real-ESRGAN, and A-ESRGAN as restorers against ResNet50 / EfficientNet-B3 / ViT-Base classifiers, with XAI benchmarking (Grad-CAM, SHAP, Integrated Gradients, attention rollout) to explain *why* restoration helps or hurts.
- **AI-generated image detection** — CLIP-based ensemble work (0.941 F1) and study of frequency-domain GAN fingerprinting, extending toward reference-free, generator-agnostic hallucination detection for medical images using reverse diffusion trajectory signals.
- **Geometric deep learning for molecules** — reading and reimplementing MPNN, SchNet, DimeNet, and NequIP as a research-expansion track into drug discovery.
